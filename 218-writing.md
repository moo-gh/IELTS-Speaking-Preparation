#### 1. Do you write a lot in your daily life?
**Quite** **a** **bit** for **study**—**notes**, **short** **paragraphs**, and **practice** **tasks** for **English**. **Personal** **writing** like **diaries** is **rarer**, but I **still** **jot** **down** **ideas** when I'm **planning** **something**.

---
#### 2. Do you enjoy writing?
**It** **depends** on the **purpose**. I **don't** **love** **long** **essays** **under** **pressure**, but I **enjoy** **expressing** **my** **thoughts** **clearly** when I **have** **enough** **time** to **edit**.

---
#### 3. What do you usually write?
**Mostly** **academic** **notes**, **messages** that **need** **more** **detail** than a **voice** **call**, and **occasional** **lists** for **tasks** or **travel**. For **IELTS**, I **also** **write** **sample** **answers** to **fix** **grammar** and **structure**.

---
#### 4. Do you prefer writing on paper or on a computer?
**A** **computer** for **anything** **long**—it's **faster** to **edit** and **easier** to **reorganise** **ideas**. **Paper** is **better** for **quick** **brainstorms** or when I want **fewer** **online** **distractions**.

---
#### 5. Did you like writing when you were at school?
**Mixed** **feelings**—**creative** **tasks** were **fun**, but **timed** **exams** **stressed** me **out**. As I **got** **older**, I **appreciated** writing **more** because it **helped** me **think** **in** a **more** **organised** **way**.

---
#### 6. Do you think good writing skills are important?
**Yes**—they **matter** in **education**, **work**, and even **everyday** **life**, like **filling** in **forms** or **explaining** a **problem** **clearly**. **Strong** **writing** **builds** **trust** and **saves** **time** for **everyone** **reading** it.
