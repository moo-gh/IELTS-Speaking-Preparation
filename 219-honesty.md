#### 1. Are you an honest person?
**I** **try** to **be**—I **don't** **like** **lying** about **important** **things** like **money**, **plans**, or **mistakes** I **made**. **Small** **social** **lies** **happen** **sometimes**, but my **default** is to **tell** the **truth** **when** it **matters**.

---
#### 2. Is it important to be honest?
**Yes**—**trust** **breaks** **quickly** when **people** **catch** **you** in a **lie**, and **rebuilding** it **takes** **time**. In **friendships**, **work**, and **family**, **honesty** **keeps** **problems** **smaller** and **easier** to **fix**.

---
#### 3. When is it difficult to tell the truth?
**When** the **truth** might **hurt** **someone's** **feelings**, like **commenting** on **appearance** or **performance**. I **still** **prefer** **kind**, **clear** **honesty** over **harsh** **criticism** or **complete** **silence**.

---
#### 4. Do you think children should always be honest?
**Mostly** **yes**—**kids** should **learn** that **lying** **about** **homework**, **rules**, or **safety** is **serious**. **Parents** can **teach** **tact** too, so **honesty** **doesn't** **mean** **being** **rude** to **everyone**.

---
#### 5. Have you ever told a white lie?
**Yes**—**small** ones like **saying** a **gift** **looks** **nice** or **claiming** I'm **not** **tired** when I **don't** want to **worry** **relatives**. They're **meant** to **protect** **feelings**, not to **cover** **real** **wrongdoing**.

---
#### 6. Do people in your country value honesty?
**In** **theory**, **yes**—**families** and **schools** **praise** **truthful** **behaviour**. **In** **practice**, **pressure**, **politeness**, and **face**-**saving** **sometimes** **push** people toward **half**-**truths**, **especially** in **formal** **situations**.
