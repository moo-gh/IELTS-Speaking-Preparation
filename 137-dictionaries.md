#### 1. Do you often use a dictionary?
**Yes**, **almost** **daily** for **English** **study**—**checking** **meanings**, **pronunciation**, and **example** **sentences** when **reading** or **writing**. **Quick** **lookups** **prevent** **small** **misunderstandings** from **snowballing** into **bigger** **mistakes**.

---
#### 2. What do you use dictionaries for?
**Vocabulary** **building**, **spelling** **checks**, **collocations**, and **confirming** **register**—whether a **word** **sounds** **formal** or **casual**. **During** **essays**, I **verify** **nuance** instead of **guessing** and **hoping** the **examiner** **won't** **notice**.

---
#### 3. What kinds of dictionaries do you think are most useful?
**Bilingual** **ones** for **beginners**, **English**-**English** **learners'** **dictionaries** for **intermediate** **levels**, and **good** **online** **apps** with **audio** for **everyone**. **Specialised** **technical** **dictionaries** **help** **only** when you're **deep** in a **field**.

---
#### 4. Do you think dictionaries are useful for learning a language?
**Essential**—they **turn** **passive** **encounters** with **new** **words** into **active** **learning** if you **look** **them** **up** **immediately**. **Without** **dictionaries**, **guessing** from **context** **works** **sometimes** but **misleads** **often**.

---
#### 5. Do you prefer paper or electronic dictionaries?
**Electronic**, **mostly**—**speed**, **portability**, **audio**, and **search** **beat** **flipping** **pages** when **deadlines** **loom**. **Paper** **dictionaries** **feel** **nice** for **slow** **study** **sessions** at **home** **without** **notifications**.

---
#### 6. What kind of information can you find in a dictionary?
**Definitions**, **pronunciation**, **word** **class**, **example** **sentences**, **synonyms**, and **sometimes** **etymology** or **usage** **notes**. **Good** **apps** **also** **link** to **related** **phrases** and **common** **mistakes** **learners** **make**.
